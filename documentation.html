Getting connected

  1. Make your audio device available to macOS. Connect your interface or start your network audio driver. Follow the device manufacturer’s setup instructions.
  2. Open Live Meters and select your input device. The device must expose its inputs through Core Audio.
  3. Allow audio input access if macOS requests it. If access was previously denied, check System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone.
  4. Choose the channels you want to monitor. Live Meters supports 1–64 input channels. Available inputs depend on your selected device.
  5. Send a known signal to an input. Check that the corresponding meter responds before relying on the display during a session.
Using network audio?

Dante Virtual Soundcard, Waves SoundGrid, and AVB devices can be used when their audio is presented through a compatible Core Audio driver. Configure network routing in the relevant device or driver software.

Reading Peak and RMS levels

Peak metering shows signal peaks and helps you spot sudden level changes. RMS metering shows average signal level. Viewing both helps you understand how a signal behaves over time.

Meter ballistics are configurable. Choose settings that make changes easy to follow for your workflow.

Live Meters reads input audio to calculate and display levels. It does not modify monitored audio or provide audio output or routing, and it does not add processing latency to the audio signal path.

Organize your meter bridge

  • Channel names: label inputs to match your console, input list, or recording session.
  • Channel colors: use a consistent color scheme to make related inputs easy to identify.
  • Display layouts: choose a layout suited to your channel count and screen space.

For a dedicated monitoring display, keep important channels visible and check that labels remain readable from your working position.

Troubleshooting

The input device is missing

Check its connection and power, and confirm that macOS recognizes it in Audio MIDI Setup. For network audio, make sure the driver is running and configured. Reopen Live Meters after the device becomes available.

The meters show no signal

Check the selected device and channels, confirm audio input permission, and verify that signal reaches those inputs. For console or network sources, check upstream routing in the console or driver software.

The wrong channels are responding

Compare your source routing with the input channels exposed by the Core Audio device. Channel names in Live Meters help identify inputs; changing a name does not change upstream routing.

Can Live Meters route or process audio?

No. Live Meters is a monitoring application. Audio routing belongs in your console, interface, driver, or recording application.
